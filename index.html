    About this way

    What we want

    • Where. Along the living-room edge of a stone-topped kitchen peninsula (that side has a bar overhang with stools), from the countertop up to a concrete beam. At the end of the peninsula the glass turns and closes the walkway into the kitchen, floor to ceiling, up to the fridge.
    • Finish. Slim matt black aluminium frames and clear tempered glass. We are open to fluted glass if you recommend it.
    • Home. A flat in Singapore. Exact sizes will be confirmed when our interior designer measures on site.
    • Nothing is built yet. The fridge, the tall unit and the cabinets can still move to suit whichever way works best.

    The three ways to put the glass away

    In all three, the screen closes the same opening: four panels along the peninsula, plus glass across the end of the peninsula and the walkway. They differ in where the glass goes when it is put away.

    1 · Folding stack on show

    Along the peninsula
    Four panels, top-hung bi-fold, from a track fixed under the beam and hidden behind a cover strip.
    Put away
    They fold into a stack against the tall cabinet at the left end of the peninsula. The stack stands on the counter, about 15 × 55 cm, held by a magnet catch.
    Right end
    Three full-height side panels, about 66 cm wide, on three tracks. They slide back beside the fridge and stack there, in the open or in a timber pocket.

    2 · Folding into a timber pocket

    Along the peninsula
    The same bi-fold as way 1.
    Put away
    The stack folds into a timber box on the end of the counter, 16 cm wide and the full 98 cm counter depth, closed by a hinged side door.
    Right end
    The corner panel is hinged to the last folding panel and folds into the pocket with the others, making five panels. The walkway door slides into a slim timber pocket beside the fridge.

    3 · Panels become the display cabinet doors

    Along the peninsula
    Four sliding panels on two parallel tracks, in synchronised pairs. The tracks run about 3.45 m under the beam, along the peninsula and on past the display cabinet.
    Put away
    The panels slide sideways and park two layers deep in front of the display cabinet, so they become its doors. The display cabinet has no doors of its own.
    Right end
    The same three side panels as way 1.

    Options for the side panels and walkway door

    These apply to whichever way we choose. We would like your view on each.

    • In the open. The side panels stack beside the fridge, on show. This is the simplest.
    • Timber pocket. A slim cabinet, about 20 cm wide, in the same wood as the kitchen, hides the parked panels. Placed beside the fridge, it stands about 20 cm into the corridor.
    • Move the fridge in. Because nothing is built yet, the fridge and the end of the peninsula could move about 20 cm inwards. The timber pocket would then sit flush with the kitchen, and the corridor keeps its full width.
    • Way 2's walkway door. A single 91 cm door needs a pocket longer than the fridge is deep (about 64 cm), so the pocket would run about 30 cm past the fridge. Two telescopic leaves would need only about half that length.

    Questions for you

    Please answer for each of the three ways. If one isn't workable, tell us why and what you would do instead.

    Track and fixing

    1. What size is the track for each way (height × width), and how many tracks side by side?
    2. Can the track be screwed to the underside of the concrete beam, with no hacking or chasing? What fixings do you use?
    3. Can the track be hidden behind a cover strip, flush with the face of the beam, so only a slim line shows? How deep must that strip be?
    4. If the beam is narrower than the tracks, or not where we think, what is your fallback? For example a slim bulkhead, or a surface track with a cover.
    5. There is probably no beam over the walkway and the corridor beside the fridge. How would you hang the side panels' tracks there?

    Panels and movement

    1. Are our panel sizes and weights within your system's limits? What glass thickness do you recommend?
    2. Is a bottom guide needed on the stone countertop? Would it be a groove cut into the stone, a surface channel, or a guide only where the panels park?
    3. Ways 1 and 2: can your bi-fold park four panels against one end and hold them with a magnet or latch? Can it take five panels, for way 2's corner panel?
    4. Way 3: can you do synchronised sliding pairs on two tracks over about 3.45 m? Can the panels slide past the corner where the peninsula meets the display cabinet? Can the parked panels work as the display cabinet's doors?
    5. Side panels: can three full-height panels, about 66 cm wide, telescope back and stack beside the fridge? How much corridor width do they and their tracks take? Could they slide into a slim timber pocket instead, and how wide must it be? For way 2's walkway door, would you use one leaf in a long pocket, or two telescopic leaves in a shorter one?

    Sealing and finish

    1. How well does each way keep cooking smells in? Where are the gaps, and which seals do you use (brush, magnetic)?
    2. Can the front glass meet the side glass at the corner without a post?
    3. How slim can the black frames be? What does fluted glass add to the price?

    What we need back

    • Which of the three ways you can build, and which you recommend
    • An itemised quote for each way you can build: glass, frames, tracks and hardware, cover strip, installation, GST
    • The hardware brand and system you would use
    • Track size and fixing method, for the beam and for the walkway side
    • Lead time from site measurement to installation
    • Warranty on glass, hardware and installation
    • Photos of similar folding or sliding glass screens you have installed
    • Whether you need a site visit before quoting, and its cost

    Still to confirm with our interior designer

    • The beam: its exact position over the peninsula edge, its width and depth, and whether two tracks and a cover strip fit under it.
    • The heights: counter, underside of the beam, and ceiling over the walkway.
    • Way 3: whether the display cabinet's front can line up exactly with the peninsula's edge, so the panels slide past the corner.
    • Whether the ceiling over the walkway and beside the fridge can carry a track, or needs a slim bulkhead.
    • Whether to give up about 20 cm of the corridor to the side panels, or move the fridge and the end of the peninsula about 20 cm inwards.
    • Whether HDB or MCST approval is needed for the tracks and the glass screen.
    • Carpentry for any timber pockets.
    • Whether the glass should stand on the cabinet line, 0.39 m further in, so the bar stays usable while the glass is closed.
